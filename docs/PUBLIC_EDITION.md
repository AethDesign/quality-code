# Public edition policy

Quality Code publishes enough for another team to adopt the standard and verify the evidence AethDesign cites. The studio maintains its delivery playbooks separately.

| Material | Where it belongs |
| --- | --- |
| Shared workflow and secret-access rules, Quality Code and Quality Visuals, generic project instructions and routes | Public. An adopter needs these to set expectations and use the quality gates. |
| Generic `audit-code-change` skill, testing and visual review references, verdict schema, installer, CLI and toolkit tests | Public. These make the advertised workflow usable and inspectable. |
| Quality Swarms instructions and detailed delegation operator guide | Internal. Published research remains available with its original evidence. |
| Studio skills, project instruction overlays, design systems, client intake, internal prompts and review playbooks, orchestration and model routing, deployment and commercial delivery | Internal. Keep them in a separate access-controlled workspace and out of the public installer, instruction packs, history and Pages releases. Keep public baselines usable on their own. |
| Case-study evidence | Release a purpose-built public version with synthetic or authorized inputs, method, aggregate results and limitations. Require explicit approval for the exact client material being released. Keep raw sessions, logs, detailed internal audits and customer data internal. |

Before releasing a file, identify the reader task or published claim it supports. Review the actual release contents, including generated archives and screenshots, for authorization, sensitive material and third-party rights. Keep enough evidence to evaluate the claim and say what a reader can reproduce. Published benchmark outputs remain historical evidence.

The [MIT license](../LICENSE) covers the public toolkit, templates and documentation. Previously published material may remain in older revisions or copies under that license. Keep internal revisions outside the public repository from their first commit.
